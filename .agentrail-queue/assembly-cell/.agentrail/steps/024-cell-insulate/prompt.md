Heat-shrink sleeving: a sleeve station (S6) feeds tubing by size (inner diameter before and after shrinking, wall, shrink temperature, as data in materials/sleeve.toml) and cuts a length; the arm (or the turntable presenting the part) slides it over a lead from its free end to a span along it -- before that end is soldered, and normally before the lead is bent; a hot-air station (or the iron's hot-air nozzle) shrinks it onto the lead (the thermal model: the sleeve must reach its shrink temperature, nearby parts warm, a joint nearby may reflow -- warnings). A sleeved span is insulated: the shorts check (clashes, leads_near) ignores contact there while collision still sees its thicker solid; an unshrunk sleeve can slide. Commands, refusals (no free end, the span past a joint or a bend it cannot pass, the wrong size), drawing (the tube, its color, shrinking), tests, a program where two crossing leads of different nets would short without it and do not with it, published. The planner (next) sleeves leads where nets cross within a clearance.
