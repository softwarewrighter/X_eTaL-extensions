Lap joints: a lead or wire attached to another lead gets its end bent (the feeder for wire, a pincer or the bend rig for a component lead) so its last 3.2 mm or more lie alongside the target lead, touching it; bring/lay bring it alongside; the joint records its overlap; the thermal model heats the overlap's mass (more overlap, more heat and dwell to wet it, the part's limit nearer); inspect judges the overlap (under 3.2 mm: weak; over a bound set by the part's heat limit: too much heat) and a right-angle butt joint is weak. A DIP leg has about 3.4 mm of straight run below its body. The planner (CellPlan.xtl) makes lap joints; the programs that solder a lead to a lead are moved to them (goldens rebased); the player draws the bent ends; tests for weak, good and overheated laps.
