A matrix product is the work a GPU does most: in a neural network's layers, in attention, in graphics. C = A x B takes a row of A and a column of B, multiplies them item by item and adds the products up – one item of C – for every row and every column. This document shows two videos of it, made from X_eTaL arrays: a small product a step at a time, and a large one as a 3D construct the camera flies through, and how every part of both comes from X_eTaL.

Purpose: what the videos show, and why

X_eTaL-gpu compiles X_eTaL to run on Apple and NVIDIA GPUs; its Accel library's ac:m_atmul is X_eTaL's inner product, '+ '* i_nner, and a GPU runs it as one work-item per item of C, in work-groups of 16 by

  1. The videos show what that means:
  2. A step at a time (15.5 s): X_eTaL-gpu's matmul demo, A [3 4] times B [4 2]. A stands on the left wall, B on the ceiling, C on the back wall, and the cube between them is every product. Item by item a row of A and a column of B light up, their four products appear in the cube with lines from their two factors, fly into C's item and become its sum. The first item is slow, the next quicker; then all six light at once – on a GPU every item is a work-item of its own.
  3. At scale (18 s): the same construct for 512 by 512 matrices: 262,144 cells a wall, 134 million multiply-adds in the cube. The camera starts far out to show the scale, flies to one work-group's 16 by 16 tile of C and the rows and columns it reads, then to one work-item: its 512 products stand as bars along a beam through the cube, and a running sum moves from the far end to the near, where it becomes one item of C. Then C fills in, a work-group at a time.

The point is the shape of the work: which items each result needs, how much there is, and how a GPU divides it up.

The chain: X_eTaL, Python, Blender, video

As in ReLU and softmax, arrays to video, nothing in the chain is written by hand for these videos:

  1. X_eTaL works out the arrays: A, B, and C by X_eTaL-gpu's Accel ac:m_atmul (pinned at GPU_COMMIT).
  2. X_eTaL works out the videos: extensions/blender/demos/Matmul.xtl and MatmulFly.xtl turn them into places, colors, keys, spans, the camera's path and the captions – the only part made for these videos.
  3. X_eTaL writes the Python: the blender extension's Video.xtl turns each table into one call of its player, every number written out.
  4. Blender runs it: bl:r_un! runs Blender in the background; the player makes the objects and keys and EEVEE renders the MP4.

3. Animation, at a high level

As in every video made with Video.xtl, the animation is a timeline of groups of objects, each group given by tables: what each object is at the start, when it is shown, where it is at each key, its color at each key; then the camera's keys and the captions. Blender only eases between the keys.

What is new here is that things move one after another: each product has keys of its own – it appears, waits, flies to C and lands at its own seconds – so its group is keyed with vd:m_ovesEach, a row of times for each object, rather than one row for the group.
